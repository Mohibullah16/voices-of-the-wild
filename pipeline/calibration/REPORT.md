# Calibration report

Generated by `npm run calibrate` on 2026-10-08. Model `onnx-community/embeddinggemma-2-ONNX`, dtype `q4`, CPU (Node), image processor defaults (280 soft tokens). Matcher: the app's own `app/src/match.ts` (CONTRACT §3), parity 253/253.

## In one paragraph

Out of 243 real photos, EmbeddingGemma 2 put the right character first 88.9% of the time with no thresholds at all. Naming that top guess every time would have meant a wrong name on 11.5% of photos. With the calibrated thresholds and the guardian fallback, the app names the right character 83.5% of the time (cross-validated). A guardian steps in 9.5% of the time, and a confidently wrong name drops to 7.0%. When it does give a name, it is right 92.3% of the time. 90.0% of the indoor negative photos got "nobody wants to talk" and 0.0% got a character name. Most of the misses are shape-alike cross-category pairs (a mosque dome vs Mazar-e-Quaid, a peepal vs a wall-peepal sapling, ants on grass vs lawn grass) rather than random noise.

## Method

- **Photos:** 243 real photos of 81/81 characters from Wikimedia Commons (CC0 / PD / CC BY / CC BY-SA, ≤768px; see [ATTRIBUTION.md](ATTRIBUTION.md)), found by scientific/common-name search. Search results were checked by eye on contact sheets: 81 obviously wrong hits (botanical drawings, the wrong subject, such as a lava fountain or zebras for "zebra crossing") were rejected in `calibration/curation.json` and replaced by the next search results. Nothing was rejected for being *hard*: distant shots, odd framing and unusual scenes stay in. 10 negative photos (indoor objects that are nobody) test the "nobody wants to talk" floor.
- **Text side:** 651 descriptions (13 guardians + 81 characters), embedded once per prefix variant. The photo is embedded with no prefix (model card: prefixes are text-only).
- **Thresholds** are fit to maximise a utility that punishes confident wrong names hardest: correct name +1, guardian hint in the right category +0.4, guardian in the wrong category -0.5, "nobody" on a real thing -1, **wrong name -1.5**; negatives: nobody +1, guardian -1, name -3. One global margin and floor. Species thresholds: three strategies were compared by cross-validation (per-category, one pooled threshold, and per-category shrunk halfway to the pooled one); the best CV utility wins. Among equally good thresholds the median is taken, so a category with no observed mistakes doesn't collapse to 0. Category thresholds equal the global floor (too few negatives per category to fit them separately).
- **Honesty check:** with ~3 photos per character, in-sample numbers flatter the thresholds. The cross-validated column refits thresholds on 2 of 3 photo folds and scores the held-out fold.
- **Caveats:** Commons photos are cleaner and better framed than phone snapshots, and these come from the internet rather than a real street. The phone runs the same q4 weights in the browser (WebGPU/wasm) while this ran on CPU in Node, and laptop/phone parity has not been measured here. Treat these numbers as an upper bound for real use.

## What's next (measured, not shipped)

- **Image anchors** (PLAN §3 mitigation 4), naive version: leave-one-out with every other Commons photo added as an extra row **lowers** raw top-1 species accuracy from 88.9% to 74.5% (category 92.6% → 86.0%). The reason is scale: a photo's best cosine to its own *text* averages 0.743, but its best cosine to *some other species' photo* averages 0.793. Image–image similarity is on a higher scale, so mixed rows let photo style (lighting, framing, background) outvote the descriptions. Anchors would need their own score track and calibration (for example image rows compared only against image rows, with a separate threshold), not just extra rows. Not shipped.
- Field photos from the actual walk (real streets) should replace Commons photos as the calibration set; Commons skews to textbook shots.
- Text side uses the same q4 weights as the phone; an fp32 text side was not compared in this run.
